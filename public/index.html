Patient Safety · Nursing Excellence · Quality

Patient Safety Observation Tool

Log a safety observation, work through the root cause, and leave the shift with a written Root Cause Statement and action plan.

Note — avoid entering patient names; use MRN/bed number per your unit's identification policy.

What did you observe?

Stick to facts you saw or measured — vitals, timing, what was/wasn't done. Save interpretation for the next step.

Category & description

Find the root cause

Use the contributing-factor categories (standard clinical RCA framework) and/or the 5 Whys. Use both if you can — they cross-check each other.

Root Cause Statement (RCS)

A good RCS names the specific, correctable cause — not just "human error" — and links it clearly to the observation. Edit the draft freely.

Action plan

Turn the root cause into corrective actions — aim for at least one action that removes the cause (e.g. process/training fix), not only one that manages this one patient.

Final report

Review, then export or print. This worksheet supplements — not replaces — Medanta's formal incident reporting system.
